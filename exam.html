1. D: Mild persistent [Asthma]
More than 2 days/week but not daily, and 3–4 nights/month = mild persistent.
2. A: Pericarditis [Other cardiac]
Pericarditis = "praying position" relief. Assess for effusion/tamponade.
3. C: Prompt ophthalmology referral to evaluate for retinoblastoma [Vision]
Leukocoria = retinoblastoma (or cataract) until proven otherwise. Absence of pain is not reassuring.
4. C: Echocardiogram with Doppler [Cardiac tests]
"Echo = Eyes (anatomy); ECG = Electricity." An ECG cannot show a hole.
5. D: Distant objects are blurry because light focuses in front of the retina [Vision]
"MY-opia: I only see what's near MY face." Hyperopia = farsighted; anisometropia = unequal; strabismus = misaligned.
6. C: Moderate persistent [Asthma]
"Worst feature wins." Night waking more than once a week (but not nightly) = moderate persistent.
7. C: Diastolic timing [Murmurs]
"Diastolic is always Dangerous." Exception to know: a venous hum is continuous and innocent.
8. B: Begin immediate, copious irrigation [Vision]
Chemical injury: irrigate FIRST. Do not delay irrigation for a history.
9. B: 7 days [Otitis media]
"10–7–5": under 2 or severe = 10 days; age 2–5 = 7 days; age 6 and older = 5–7 days.
10. A: Gentle nasal suction, hydration support, and monitoring [Other respiratory]
Bronchiolitis = 3 S's: Suction, Sips, Saturation. No routine albuterol, steroids, or antibiotics.
11. D: Hold sports clearance and arrange a cardiac evaluation [PDA/obstructive]
"Faints while running = Referral." Consider aortic stenosis or HCM.
12. A: Send a backup throat culture [Strep pharyngitis]
A negative rapid antigen test in a symptomatic child aged 3 or older needs a backup throat culture. ASO shows past strep, not acute infection.
13. A: Pain control with observation and follow-up in 48–72 hours, or antibiotics, by shared decision [Otitis media]
Age 2+ and nonsevere → observation or antibiotics. ALWAYS treat the pain.
14. B: Keep the child calm with the parent and prepare for emergency airway care [Croup]
Epiglottitis = 4 D's (Drooling, Dysphagia, Dysphonia, Distress). Do not inspect the throat. Airway comes first.
15. A: Gonococcal conjunctivitis needing urgent systemic ceftriaxone [Vision]
"Gonorrhea Goes fast" (days 2–5); it threatens the cornea. "Chlamydia Comes later" (days 5–12, oral treatment).
16. A: Bacterial tracheitis [Croup]
High fever, toxicity, and poor response to standard croup treatment suggest bacterial tracheitis.
17. B: Continuous machinery murmur with bounding pulses [PDA/obstructive]
PDA = "machinery." Wide pulse pressure and bounding pulses.
18. D: Assess respiratory rate and auscultate the lungs [Pneumonia]
Lower-lobe pneumonia can present as belly pain. Always examine the lungs in a febrile child with abdominal complaints.
19. C: Prompt specialist referral for bronchoscopy [Foreign body]
"Sudden + one Side" = foreign body. Food is radiolucent, so a normal x-ray does not rule it out.
20. A: Antibiotics [Otitis media]
Under 2 years with both ears affected → antibiotics, even when nonsevere.
21. A: Avoid contact sports until the spleen is evaluated [Strep complications]
"Front nodes = Strep, Back nodes = Mono." An enlarged spleen can rupture, so avoid contact sports.
22. C: Mastoiditis requiring urgent evaluation [Otitis media]
Postauricular swelling and a protruding pinna = mastoiditis → urgent hospital/ENT care.
23. B: After 5 full days of effective antibiotics [Pertussis]
"5 days on drugs, 21 days without" (untreated: 21 days after cough onset).
24. A: Wait at least 1 month after stopping before giving MMR [Immunizations]
Steroid "2–20–14": 2 mg/kg/day or 20 mg/day or more for 14 days or more → delay live vaccines at least 1 month.
25. B: Right peritonsillar abscess [Strep complications]
The uvula runs AWAY from the abscess. Deviation to the left means the abscess is on the right.
26. B: Aortic root dilation and dissection [Other cardiac]
Marfan → aortic root aneurysm/dissection, plus possible MVP.
27. A: Pertussis [Pertussis]
Infants with pertussis often have apnea and cyanosis WITHOUT a whoop. Look for a coughing contact.
28. B: Report for reassessment of complications, adherence, dose, or another diagnosis [Pneumonia]
No improvement in 48–72 hours → reassess for empyema/effusion, the wrong organism, adherence, or a foreign body.
29. B: Mitral (and aortic) valves [Rheumatic fever]
Rheumatic = Valves. Kawasaki = Coronaries.
30. A: Atrial septal defect [Cyanotic CHD]
TOF = PROV: Pulmonary stenosis, RVH, Overriding aorta, VSD (ventricular, not atrial).
31. B: Report mood or behavior changes, including suicidal thoughts [Asthma]
"Montelukast = Mood warning" (boxed warning). It is not a rescue medication.
32. D: Sit up, lean forward, and pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 minutes [School and teaching]
Lean FORWARD and pinch the soft alae continuously for at least 10 minutes. Never tilt back.
33. C: Continue with dose 3; do not restart the series [Immunizations]
Catch-up: NEVER restart a series. DTaP is used under 7 years.
34. D: Coarctation of the aorta [PDA/obstructive]
Coarctation = "Strong upstairs, weak downstairs."
35. D: 80–90 mg/kg/day divided BID [Otitis media]
High-dose amoxicillin for AOM differs from the GAS regimen (50 mg/kg once daily).
36. A: Place the infant in a knee-chest position [Cyanotic CHD]
Tet spell → knee-chest (raises systemic resistance and pushes blood to the lungs), calm the infant, give O2, call for help.
37. C: Wide, fixed split S2 [ASD/VSD]
ASD → "Always Split, Doesn't change." Increased right-sided flow delays pulmonic valve closure.
38. C: Risk of Reye syndrome [Medications]
Reye syndrome risk. Kawasaki disease is a specialist-directed exception.
39. A: Refer, because the passing threshold at age 4 is 20/40 [Vision]
AAPOS: age 3 = 20/50, age 4 = 20/40, age 5+ = 20/32. "Child grows, number shrinks."
40. D: Low-dose inhaled corticosteroid [Asthma]
Mild persistent = Step 2: daily low-dose ICS. "ICS = Insurance."
41. C: Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy [Murmurs]
HCM gets louder with standing/Valsalva (less filling). Most innocent murmurs get softer.
42. C: 1.2 million units [Strep pharyngitis]
27 kg is the dividing line: under 27 kg = 600,000 units; 27 kg or more = 1.2 million units.
43. C: Observe for 2–4 hours for return of symptoms [Croup]
Epinephrine works fast but wears off. "Watch 2–4 hours before the door."
44. C: Azithromycin [Pneumonia]
"My School = Mycoplasma." A gradual, atypical pattern in a school-age child is treated with a macrolide.
45. A: Coronary artery aneurysm [Kawasaki]
"Kawasaki Crowns the Coronaries." (Rheumatic fever targets valves.)
46. A: Yellow zone [Asthma]
180/300 = 60%. Green is 80% or more, yellow 50–79%, red under 50%.
47. D: Moderate to severe bulging of the tympanic membrane [Otitis media]
"Bulge = AOM. Red alone = Rubbish." Crying reddens the TM, and tugging is nonspecific.
48. D: Sweating and tiring during feeds, with poor weight gain [CHD basics]
"Feeding is the infant's treadmill." Peripheral edema is less prominent than in adults.
49. B: Chlamydia trachomatis [Pneumonia]
Chlamydia's 3 C's: Choppy (staccato) cough, Cool (afebrile), Conjunctivitis first. Classic at 1–3 months.
50. D: Reduces the risk of acute rheumatic fever [Strep pharyngitis]
"Penicillin protects the Heart, not the Kidney." Treatment prevents ARF; antibiotics do not reliably prevent PSGN.
51. C: Acute rheumatic fever [Rheumatic fever]
JONES: 2 majors (Joints + carditis) plus evidence of prior strep = ARF.
52. C: A single dose of oral dexamethasone [Croup]
"Dex for all." Mild croup = dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg once. Croup is a clinical diagnosis.
53. B: Staphylococcus aureus, including MRSA [Pneumonia]
After the flu → think Staph (including MRSA) and pneumococcus.
54. A: Still give 2 routine MMR doses starting at 12 months [Immunizations]
A dose before 12 months does not count toward the routine 2-dose series.
55. C: Pain with eye movement and proptosis [Eye]
Orbital = 4 P's: Pain with movement, Proptosis, Poor vision, Paralysis (restricted movement) → emergency IV antibiotics.
56. C: Ventricular septal defect [ASD/VSD]
VSD = "Very loud." And a small VSD can be loud: "Small hole, loud howl."
57. D: Kawasaki disease [Kawasaki]
CRASH and BURN: fever 5+ days plus 4 of 5 (Conjunctivitis, Rash, Adenopathy, Strawberry tongue, Hands/feet).
58. B: The ears, for concurrent otitis media [Eye]
Bacterial conjunctivitis + AOM (often nontypeable H. flu) → amoxicillin-clavulanate.
59. D: Grade IV [Murmurs]
"4 = you can FEEL it." Grade III is loud without a thrill; VI is heard with the stethoscope off the chest.
60. D: All lesions are crusted over [School and teaching]
Varicella: all lesions crusted. A fixed number of days alone is not enough.
61. B: Holter monitor [Cardiac tests]
"Holter = Hold on for a day" (frequent symptoms). "Event = Every now and then" (rare symptoms).
62. D: Tdap, MenACWY, and HPV [Immunizations]
"Teens Meet HPV." HPV may start at age 9; MenACWY booster at 16.
63. A: 11 months [Kawasaki]
"2 grams → wait 11 months." Non-live vaccines do not need to wait.
64. B: Chalazion; warm compresses and lid hygiene [Eye]
"A Stye Stings, a Chalazion is Chill." A chalazion is sterile, so antibiotics are not routine. Never squeeze.
65. A: DTaP, IPV, Hib, PCV, and rotavirus [Immunizations]
2, 4, 6 months: "Dad Is Having Pizza Rolls" = DTaP, IPV, Hib, PCV, Rotavirus (plus HepB timing).
66. B: Tetralogy of Fallot [CHD basics]
Cyanotic = the 5 T's. The "D's" (ASD, VSD, PDA) are acyanotic left-to-right shunts.
67. A: 500 mg per dose, twice daily [Dose calculation]
"Per DOSE → Don't divide." 25 × 20 = 500 mg each dose (max 500 mg/dose).
68. D: Poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis [Strep complications]
PSGN = "Cola, Puffy, Pressure": dark urine, edema, and high BP after strep.
69. A: DTaP, IPV, MMR, and varicella [Immunizations]
School entry = "DIMV."
70. B: Give both vaccines as scheduled [Immunizations]
Household immunocompromise is not a reason to withhold MMR/varicella. If a varicella rash develops, avoid direct contact until it resolves.
71. B: A child with a prosthetic heart valve [Other cardiac]
Prophylaxis is limited to the highest-risk patients (prosthetic valve, prior IE, selected cyanotic/repaired CHD).
72. C: Increased left atrial pressure [Fetal circulation]
Lungs expand → PVR falls, cord clamping raises systemic resistance → left atrial pressure rises and pushes the flap closed.
73. B: Hearing loss from middle-ear fluid (OME) [Ear and hearing]
Conductive = Cerumen and fluid (outer/middle ear). Sensorineural = Snail (cochlea) and nerve.
74. B: High-dose amoxicillin, about 90 mg/kg/day divided BID [Pneumonia]
Typical bacterial pneumonia (S. pneumoniae) → amoxicillin. A macrolide is not an automatic substitute.
75. A: Right atrium to left atrium [Fetal circulation]
Foramen Ovale = "Open door between the atria" (RA → LA). Ductus arteriosus = PA → aorta. Ductus venosus bypasses the liver.
76. B: A button battery [Ear and hearing]
Never irrigate the 3 B's: Battery, Beans (vegetable matter), Broken eardrum. A battery requires urgent removal.
77. D: Still's (innocent) murmur [Murmurs]
Innocent = 7 S's. Still's is vibratory/musical and louder supine.
78. A: Rinse the mouth and spit after each use [Asthma]
Rinse and spit reduces thrush. ICS is used daily. Dry-powder inhalers do NOT use spacers.
79. B: MMR and varicella [Immunizations]
The "live birthday party" at 12–15 months: MMR + varicella (plus Hib/PCV boosters and HepA).
80. A: This is not contagious; use cool compresses and allergy eye drops [Eye]
Allergic = Itchy. Not contagious. Reduce exposure; antihistamine/mast-cell stabilizer drops.
81. D: Arthralgia cannot be counted when arthritis is already a major criterion [Rheumatic fever]
Don't double-count: arthritis + arthralgia, or carditis + prolonged PR.
82. C: Access-site bleeding and pulses distal to the site [Cardiac tests]
After a cath, monitor the site for bleeding and check distal pulses and perfusion.
83. C: Give the scheduled vaccines today [Immunizations]
Mild illness and antibiotics are not contraindications. Moderate/severe illness is a precaution.
84. B: 10.3 mL [Dose calculation]
22 × 15 = 330 mg. 330 × 5 ÷ 160 = 10.3 mL.
85. D: Supportive care; antibiotics are not routinely indicated [Other respiratory]
Acute bronchitis is usually viral. Purulent sputum alone does not justify antibiotics.
86. A: Indomethacin or ibuprofen [PDA/obstructive]
"Indomethacin Interrupts (closes); Prostaglandin keeps it Patent."
87. D: 900 mg [Dose calculation]
44 ÷ 2.2 = 20 kg. 20 × 90 = 1,800 mg/day ÷ 2 = 900 mg per dose. "Per DAY → Divide."
88. A: Another dose, because doses less than 5 months apart are not valid for the 2-dose series [Immunizations]
Started before 15 = 2 doses 6–12 months apart; if less than 5 months apart, a third dose is needed.
89. C: 9 mL [Dose calculation]
18 × 10 = 180 mg. 180 × 5 ÷ 100 = 9 mL.
90. D: Amoxicillin-clavulanate [Otitis media]
Augmentin 3 C's: amox in the last 30 days, Conjunctivitis plus otitis (nontypeable H. flu), Can't clear.
91. C: Paroxysmal [Pertussis]
"Cold → Cough → Calm" = Catarrhal → Paroxysmal → Convalescent.
92. D: Impending respiratory failure [Asthma]
"A silent chest is a scream." Less wheeze with worse air entry and fatigue means respiratory failure.
93. B: Do not start rotavirus; the first dose must be given by 14 weeks 6 days [Immunizations]
Rotavirus: start by 14 weeks 6 days ("by 15 weeks"), finish by 8 months.
94. C: It is not valid; injectable live vaccines must be given the same day or at least 28 days apart [Immunizations]
"Live shots: same day or wait a month (28 days)." The second live vaccine given too early should be repeated.
95. B: Pulmonary vascular resistance fell, increasing left-to-right shunting [ASD/VSD]
As pulmonary resistance drops over the first weeks, more blood crosses to the lungs → heart failure.
96. D: Prostaglandin E1 to keep the ductus open [Cyanotic CHD]
TGA = "Two separate circles" that need mixing. Never close that PDA.
97. D: IVIG 2 g/kg plus aspirin [Kawasaki]
IVIG within the first 10 days reduces coronary complications. Kawasaki is the exception to avoiding aspirin.
98. C: Daytime cough and nasal drainage for more than 10 days without improvement [Other respiratory]
"10 days, Double-dip, 39 for 3." Colored mucus alone is not enough.
99. D: Cough, rhinorrhea, hoarseness, and oral ulcers [Strep pharyngitis]
"Looks like a COLD → not strep." Exudate alone does not prove GAS because EBV and other viruses can cause it.
100. C: Watchful waiting for about 3 months, with a hearing check if it persists [Ear and hearing]
OME = "Fluid without Fire." No antibiotics, antihistamines, decongestants, or steroids.
