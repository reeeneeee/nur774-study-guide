Assessment

Question 1 of 50 SINGLE ANSWER

Which history finding in a 2-month-old most suggests a heart problem?

  1. Sleeping about 16 hours a day
  2. Sweating and tiring during feedings
  3. Spitting up small amounts after feeds
  4. Six wet diapers a day
Tap to show answer

✅ B

  • Sweating and tiring during feedings

Why: Feeding is hard work for an infant. Diaphoresis, fatigue, and long feeds signal heart failure. The others are normal infant findings.

🧠 "Feeding is the infant's treadmill." Sweaty, tired feeder = check the heart.

VSD

Question 5 of 50 SINGLE ANSWER

Symptoms of a large VSD usually appear at what age?

  1. 6 months
  2. 4–8 weeks
  3. The first hour of life
  4. School age
Tap to show answer

✅ B

  • 4–8 weeks

Why: Pulmonary resistance falls over the first weeks, so more blood shunts left → right and the infant becomes symptomatic around 4–8 weeks.

🧠 "4 to 8, the lungs relax and the VSD floods them."

🧠 "VSD = Very loud." Small hole, loud howl.
Question 7 of 50 SELECT ALL THAT APPLY

Which statements about a ventricular septal defect are correct?

  1. It increases pulmonary blood flow
  2. It always needs emergency prostaglandin E1
  3. Many small VSDs close on their own
  4. VSDs that do not close may need surgery
  5. It is an opening between the ventricles
  6. It causes cyanosis at birth from right-to-left flow

✅ A, C, D, E

  • It increases pulmonary blood flow
  • Many small VSDs close on their own

Why: A VSD is a left-to-right (acyanotic) shunt. Most small ones close spontaneously; persistent significant ones need surgical closure.

🧠 "Small hole: wait and watch. Big hole: fix it."

ASD

Question 9 of 50 SINGLE ANSWER

Which heart sound is the classic sign of an atrial septal defect?

  1. Continuous machinery murmur
  2. Fixed splitting of the second heart sound (S2)
  3. Harsh holosystolic murmur
  4. Diastolic murmur at the apex
Tap to show answer

✅ B

  • Fixed splitting of the second heart sound (S2)

Why: Extra right-sided flow delays pulmonic valve closure, so S2 is widely split and doesn't change with breathing.

🧠 "ASD = Always Split, Doesn't change."

PDA

Question 11 of 50 SINGLE ANSWER

The ductus arteriosus connects which two structures?

  1. Pulmonary artery and aorta
  2. Right and left ventricles
  3. Umbilical vein and inferior vena cava
  4. Right and left atria
Tap to show answer

✅ A

  • Pulmonary artery and aorta

Why: In the fetus it lets blood skip the lungs. It should start closing with the first breaths (the addendum says closure is complete by about 3 months).

🧠 "Ductus ARTERIOSUS connects the two big ARTERIES."

Pulmonic stenosis

Question 15 of 50 SINGLE ANSWER

Pulmonic stenosis causes hypertrophy of which chamber?

  1. Right ventricle
  2. Left atrium

🧠 "Pulmonic = Pushes the Right side."

Coarctation

Question 22 of 50 SINGLE ANSWER

Which finding is classic for coarctation of the aorta?

  1. Higher blood pressure in the arms than the legs
  2. Continuous machinery murmur
  3. Higher blood pressure in the legs than the arms
  4. Fixed split S2
Tap to show answer

✅ A

  • Higher blood pressure in the arms than the legs

Why: The narrowing blocks flow to the lower body: pressure is high above it and low below it, with weak or absent femoral pulses.

🧠 "Strong upstairs, weak downstairs."

Tap to show answer

✅ B, C, D, F

  • Leg pain with activity
  • Headache
  • Nosebleeds
  • Dizziness (vertigo)

Why: High pressure in the upper body causes headache, vertigo, and nosebleeds (even stroke); poor flow to the legs causes leg pain.

🧠 "Head, Nose, Legs": high pressure up top (headache, nosebleed), low flow down below (leg pain).

Tricuspid atresia

Question 26 of 50 SINGLE ANSWER

In tricuspid atresia, how does blood leave the right atrium?

🧠 "Front door locked? Take the side door" (the foramen ovale).

Heart failure

Question 35 of 50 SELECT ALL THAT APPLY

Which are signs of RIGHT-sided heart failure?

  1. Hepatomegaly
  2. Crackles in the lungs
  3. Increased venous pressure
  4. Dyspnea as the first sign
  5. Edema/weight gain from fluid
Tap to show answer

✅ A, C, E

  • Hepatomegaly
  • Increased venous pressure
  • Edema/weight gain from fluid

Why: Right-sided failure backs fluid up into the body (liver, veins). Left-sided failure backs up into the LUNGS (crackles, dyspnea).

🧠 "Left = Lungs. Right = the Rest of the body" (liver).

  • Crackles (rales) in the lungs

🧠 "Left = Lungs."
